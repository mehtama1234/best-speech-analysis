## 2. Rowling: the title does not contain the full argument

### Benefits are presented without foreknown success

The family-expectations account is qualified by responsibility for later choices
and a rejection of romanticized poverty. The personal failure account also
explicitly denies that a favorable outcome was visible at the time. Only then
does it identify retrospective benefits: narrowed priorities, retained resources
and relationships.

Those limits must remain attached to the story. It does not establish that
hardship is desirable, necessary for growth or a reliable route to success.
Broad psychological generalizations in the source remain unverified; the
speaker's retrospective account is not a controlled comparison.

### A capacity can be useful without being inherently good

theme. These are descriptions of testimony and experience, not sources the
assistant has independently examined. No graphic elaboration is needed to
preserve their role in the argument.

The address later explicitly says the capacity can also manipulate or control.
That qualification prevents a simplistic equation of imagination with goodness.
Organizational impact and claims about other people's experiences remain
attributed, not measured outcomes.
