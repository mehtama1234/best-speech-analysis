## Why this matters for practice

The same wording can frame a value judgment, prediction, recommendation or
reported statement. A person practicing clear qualification should identify
the claim being qualified, preserve its conditions, and attribute reported
views. These are practice hypotheses grounded in textual distinctions, not
measured audience benefits. Merely adding “I think” is not a demonstrated
technique for sounding cautious or trustworthy.

The practice card now exposes contextual subtype, attribution and reasons for
its examples. Its limitation explicitly states that reported words and
unresolved turns must not be treated as the primary speaker's uncertainty.
The report also labels inherited confidence values as legacy judgments rather
than confidence newly assigned by this review.
