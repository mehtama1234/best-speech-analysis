## Candidate mechanism: a repeated stem can change function

The catalogue in captions 31–44 repeatedly introduces what a participant
believes. The existing retrieval rules can flag this wording as qualification.
In this passage, however, many items state tastes and commitments rather than
reduce certainty about a factual proposition. A repeated grammatical stem
organizes a heterogeneous list and presents a stance in the dialogue.

This is a candidate interpretation, not a general finding. A list of beliefs
can still contain factual assertions or policy-like wishes; those contents must
be separated rather than treating every item as either hedging or certainty.
The asserted historical and scientific claims are not endorsed. Garbled names
and joined words in the source are preserved rather than silently repaired.

There is also a distinction between local and episode-level function. A question
or isolated preference can be intelligible by itself, but the sequence shows
how a challenge leads to a stance declaration and then a renewed exchange.
That sequence is invisible to a detector operating on isolated caption words.

## What to test next

Look for repeated-stem catalogues elsewhere in independently selected passages.
Separate self-characterization, persuasion, qualification and explanation, and
compare passages where similar wording serves a different function. Keep film
dialogue distinct from spontaneous interviews and prepared public addresses.
Do not assume a scripted scene transfers directly to live-speaking practice.

A proposed learner exercise is to label what each item in a repeated-stem list
does: preference, value, factual claim or proposed rule. Then ask whether the
repetition clarifies a coherent position or merely groups unlike statements.
This is an analysis exercise, not evidence that the technique improves impact.
