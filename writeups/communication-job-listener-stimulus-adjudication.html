Listener stimulus adjudication

This form records the adjudicator’s explicit lock/revise/exclude decision after two distinct independent stimulus reviews. A lock is a pilot-readiness decision only; it is not listener outcome evidence, communication-effectiveness evidence or proof of equivalence.
Adjudicator record
1. opening · draft-opening-01

Proposition: The library will change weekend hours next month and visitors need the new schedule.

Control: Next month, the library will change its weekend hours. The new schedule will be posted at the entrance and online. Visitors should check it before coming.

Pattern-informed: Next month, the library will change its weekend hours. I will explain what is changing, why visitors need to know, and where to check the schedule before coming.

Pattern-informed: The garden needs a watering rota. Volunteers can sign up for a morning or evening slot on the shared sheet, and we will review it next Friday. The takeaway is simple: choose one slot only if you can keep it.

Pattern-informed: A clear reminder may reduce missed appointments. For example, one clinic sent a reminder the day before and recorded fewer missed visits that month. The case illustrates a possibility, but it does not prove the reminder caused the change everywhere.

Pattern-informed: We expected the order in two days, but it arrived in five after a supplier delay. The change explains why the estimate did not hold; it does not by itself show that anyone acted carelessly. For the next order, we should check the supplier date first.
