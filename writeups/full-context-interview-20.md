## Move from priority to an account of how time is allocated

At 118–139, the host's favorable portrait prompts a work-family balance question.
At 140–173, the guest supplies a schedule: production days, concentrated working
hours, weekend limits and annual family holidays. These details make the proposed
routine more specific than the repeated priority slogan alone.

The schedule is self-reported, not independently observed time-use data. It also
depends on circumstances that are not quantified here: role, resources, agreement
with collaborators and control over the calendar. Its presence does not establish
that every worker or founder can adopt the same arrangement.

At 174–194, the explanation shifts into an assurance that setting priorities and
being predictably present means nothing can go wrong. That is much stronger than
the evidence offered. Personal experience and reports of changes in colleagues
cannot justify a universal guarantee. Family-first language also must not become
advice to maintain unsafe relationships or ignore other legitimate obligations.

## The interviewer proposes an explanation that the guest disputes

At 195–222, questions about team discipline receive an answer about agreements.
The host suggests that the actor's position enables compliance. The guest rejects
that explanation as exclusive and says smaller producers can do it too, then
distinguishes being an actor under a producer from being his own producer.

This exchange records a disagreement about the source of authority; it does not
settle it. Neither contractual enforceability nor equal bargaining power across
large and small producers is established. A summary should preserve the host's
hypothesis and the guest's response rather than adopt either as demonstrated fact.

## The last answer does not endorse the message requested

At 223–235, the host asks for a message stressing that making money matters early
in a business. The guest responds with earlier fees and a long career account,
then at 248–256 advises pursuing product and quality before money. He explicitly
ties that advice to how he believes his own career developed.

This is a redirection, not agreement with the requested money-first message.
Reported earnings, ranking and the causal role of quality remain unverified;
the passage is not current financial or startup guidance. Music and a final
fragment follow at 257–259.
