## Responsibility allows different roles and limited results

The parenting question leads to age-scaled duties and responsibility as privilege
(269–328). The answer then explicitly allows different forms of contribution:
technical work, caregiving and mentoring need not all become public speaking
or protest leadership (329–353). A leadership corpus should preserve this
boundary rather than treating stage presence as the universal route to impact.

The persistence section is equally specific about limits (354–385). Early
organizing is credited with parks and programs, while wholesale transformation
of those communities is explicitly denied. The point is experience that can
be built upon despite continuing problems, not a retrospective account in which
everything was solved. That distinction makes this a useful comparison against
unqualified success assurances elsewhere in the corpus.
