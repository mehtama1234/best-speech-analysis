## A borrowed story supplies the first framework

Thanks and a former-student identification precede a disclaimer about speech
length (0–8). Anticipating boredom does not establish that the audience is bored.
At 9–22, a woman in a story describes repeated difficulty. The speaker explicitly
introduces this as a story heard at school, not as her own witnessed experience.

At 23–45, a mother uses three pots containing different materials. The shared
boiling condition and different narrated results lead to a mapping onto responses
to adversity (46–58): weakening, hardening and changing one's environment.
This is a moral analogy, not an experiment conducted at the event or a validated
exhaustive classification of people. The intended audience is asked to choose
the favored response. No reply is retained.

At 59–69, the story is explicitly transferred to students entering a new stage:
contribute ideas and improve surroundings. That bridge matters more than simply
counting a story as present. It tells listeners which part of the analogy the
speaker wants them to apply. It does not prove that the proposed response is
available, sufficient or effective under every kind of adversity.

## Personal agency becomes a collective appeal

At 70–91, demographic claims support a collective opportunity argument. The
national frame expands to a global one after a reference to foreign students.
Largest youth population, youngest country and share categorized as youth are
different statistical claims, not equivalent quantities; dates and denominators
are unverified. The international-student count is also source-attributed.

The contrast between complaint and change returns to the story's opening problem.
It should not be promoted into a rule that raising concerns is unproductive or
that people are responsible for overcoming every structural constraint themselves.
The transcript supplies exhortation, not an evaluated intervention.

## The second list is not a continuation of the first taxonomy

At 92–114, parental advice introduces a separate three-part structure:

| Advice | Role in the argument | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sincerity and honesty toward a goal | Standard for effort regardless of perceived goal size | Claim that sincerity guarantees achievement is unsupported |
| Goodness despite wealth or power | Moral definition of success | Not a measured success criterion |
| Happiness in one's work | Desired ultimate outcome | Aspiration is not evidence that happiness is always available |

These values do not correspond one-to-one with carrot, egg and coffee. Treating
both lists as a single three-step method would invent relationships absent from
the text. The title foregrounds the values, while much of the address develops
the adversity parable and collective contribution argument.

The sincerity-to-achievement assurance at 100–101 is especially important to
retain as a limitation. An appealing closing principle does not establish its
causal sufficiency or remove the role of resources, chance and external barriers.

## The final contrast emphasizes application

At 115–118, learned material is contrasted with what graduates make of it.
Within this context, the point is application, not a demonstrated claim that
education has no value. Thanks close the address. The resource pitch at 119–127
belongs to the publisher and must not be counted as speaker advice.

## Candidate patterns and next checks

Candidates are a borrowed story with an explicit audience-transfer sentence,
an opening complaint frame returned to as contribution, and a national appeal
broadened to include other students. The separate lists are a useful test case
for whether an annotation system preserves local structure rather than merging
all numbered items into one framework.

A provisional exercise is to explain exactly what listeners should carry from
a story into their situation, while stating where the analogy stops. Test whether
they distinguish analogy from proof. No practice card or effectiveness claim is
admitted here; the unsupported success guarantee remains a counterexample to
unqualified motivational advice.
