Provisional communication-job cards

Practice hypotheses, not proven best practices. All examples are transcript-derived; delivery, independent review, learner and listener gates remain visible.

opening

Problem: Give the listener a reason and orientation for the exchange.

Mechanism hypothesis: Hypothesis: an opening can reduce orientation cost by naming the situation, relationship or question before detail arrives.

Repeatable form: Name the room or question; state why it matters; preview the route.

Inventory: 90 records / 79 videos

Practice: Open the same two-minute explanation with context, then without it; ask a listener what they think the talk is for.

Self-check: Can a listener state the situation and purpose without guessing?

When not to use: Do not force a formal opening into a fast response or an already shared conversation.

closing

Problem: End without leaving the main point or next step diffuse.

Mechanism hypothesis: Hypothesis: a bounded close can compress the route and give the listener a retrievable takeaway, but repetition alone is not evidence of understanding.

Repeatable form: Return to the point; name the limit or next step; release the turn.

Inventory: 124 records / 111 videos

Practice: Close a short explanation in one sentence, then ask a listener to restate the takeaway and what remains open.

Self-check: Does the close add orientation rather than merely announce that the talk is ending?

When not to use: Do not close over a live objection that still needs an answer.

question

Problem: Invite inquiry, reflection or a useful next turn.

Mechanism hypothesis: Hypothesis: a question can allocate attention and make the next reasoning step visible, but its usefulness depends on answerability and consent.

Repeatable form: Ask one specific question; give context; allow a genuine answer or opt-out.

Inventory: 141 records / 82 videos

Practice: Replace a broad question with a specific, answerable one and compare the next turn.

Self-check: Could the listener answer, decline or ask for clarification without penalty?

When not to use: Do not use a rhetorical question to disguise a demand or corner someone.

answer_or_response

Problem: Respond to the literal prompt while addressing the underlying need.

Mechanism hypothesis: Hypothesis: naming the frame and then answering can reduce mismatch between what was asked and what the listener needs, but reframing can also evade the question.

Repeatable form: Answer the literal point; state a useful frame if needed; give the bounded answer.

Inventory: 34 records / 31 videos

Practice: Give a direct answer, then a frame-corrected answer; ask which one remains responsive.

Self-check: Can the listener identify both the direct answer and any justified reframing?

When not to use: Do not reframe merely to avoid an uncomfortable but legitimate question.

story_or_personal_experience

Problem: Make an idea concrete without turning one life into universal proof.

Mechanism hypothesis: Hypothesis: time, place and action can give an abstract point human and temporal scale; an explicit boundary can prevent overgeneralization.

Repeatable form: Give a bounded scene; say what it changed or illustrates; state what it cannot prove.

Inventory: 184 records / 121 videos

Practice: Tell a 45-second event, then name the narrow lesson and one limit.

Self-check: Can the listener separate the event, the interpretation and the limit?

When not to use: Do not disclose another person’s private experience or use trauma as decorative evidence.

example_or_case

Problem: Give a general claim a concrete handle without presenting an illustration as proof.

Mechanism hypothesis: Hypothesis: a concrete case can make an abstract claim inspectable; returning to the claim helps distinguish illustration from evidence.

Repeatable form: State the claim; give one representative case; return to the claim and its evidence boundary.

Inventory: 75 records / 60 videos

Practice: Explain a principle with and without a case; ask which detail made the idea more inspectable.

Self-check: Does the case clarify the claim without pretending to establish its prevalence or cause?

When not to use: Do not select a vivid outlier to imply a typical pattern without denominators.

contrast_or_disagreement

Problem: Make a distinction or change in reasoning visible without escalating disagreement.

Mechanism hypothesis: Hypothesis: naming both sides can reduce the listener’s need to infer the replacement claim, but contrast can oversimplify real alternatives.

Repeatable form: Name the earlier view; identify what changed; state the present view and remaining overlap.

Inventory: 87 records / 72 videos

Practice: Use ‘I used to think X; Y changed the question; now I think Z,’ then test whether the distinction is fair.

Self-check: Can the listener state what changed without turning the other side into a caricature?

When not to use: Do not manufacture a false binary or treat a person as an argument to defeat.

correction_or_reframe

Problem: Move from an unhelpful starting frame to a more useful question or category.

Mechanism hypothesis: Hypothesis: an explicit frame correction can prevent downstream reasoning from inheriting a false premise, but it can feel evasive if the original concern is ignored.

Repeatable form: Acknowledge the starting frame; explain the correction; answer the more useful question.

Inventory: 74 records / 61 videos

Repeatable form: Describe the event; name the value it raises; distinguish interpretation from verified fact.

Inventory: 92 records / 71 videos

Practice: Take one personal or public event and state the value interpretation plus one alternative reading.

Self-check: Can the listener tell which part is observation, value judgment and open question?

When not to use: Do not use a value label to end inquiry or claim moral superiority.
