# Provisional communication-job cards

Status: **transcript-derived practice hypotheses; not validated**.

These cards organize transcript-derived observations into practice hypotheses. They do not establish that a form works, recurs independently, improves delivery or changes listener outcomes.

## `opening`

**Problem:** Give the listener a reason and orientation for the exchange.

**Mechanism hypothesis:** Hypothesis: an opening can reduce orientation cost by naming the situation, relationship or question before detail arrives.

**Repeatable form:** Name the room or question; state why it matters; preview the route.

**Recurrence inventory:** 90 records across 79 videos.

**Practice:** Open the same two-minute explanation with context, then without it; ask a listener what they think the talk is for.

**Self-check:** Can a listener state the situation and purpose without guessing?

**When not to use:** Do not force a formal opening into a fast response or an already shared conversation.

**Delivery evidence:** 0 internally reviewed sample(s); status `no_aligned_internal_sample_for_this_job`.

## `closing`

**Problem:** End without leaving the main point or next step diffuse.

**Mechanism hypothesis:** Hypothesis: a bounded close can compress the route and give the listener a retrievable takeaway, but repetition alone is not evidence of understanding.

**Repeatable form:** Return to the point; name the limit or next step; release the turn.

**Recurrence inventory:** 124 records across 111 videos.

**Practice:** Close a short explanation in one sentence, then ask a listener to restate the takeaway and what remains open.

**Self-check:** Does the close add orientation rather than merely announce that the talk is ending?

**When not to use:** Do not close over a live objection that still needs an answer.

**Delivery evidence:** 0 internally reviewed sample(s); status `no_aligned_internal_sample_for_this_job`.

## `question`

**Problem:** Invite inquiry, reflection or a useful next turn.

**Mechanism hypothesis:** Hypothesis: a question can allocate attention and make the next reasoning step visible, but its usefulness depends on answerability and consent.

**Repeatable form:** Ask one specific question; give context; allow a genuine answer or opt-out.

**Recurrence inventory:** 141 records across 82 videos.

**Practice:** Replace a broad question with a specific, answerable one and compare the next turn.

**Self-check:** Could the listener answer, decline or ask for clarification without penalty?

**When not to use:** Do not use a rhetorical question to disguise a demand or corner someone.

**Delivery evidence:** 10 internally reviewed sample(s); status `internal_bounded_review_not_independent`.

## `answer_or_response`

**Problem:** Respond to the literal prompt while addressing the underlying need.

**Mechanism hypothesis:** Hypothesis: naming the frame and then answering can reduce mismatch between what was asked and what the listener needs, but reframing can also evade the question.

**Repeatable form:** Answer the literal point; state a useful frame if needed; give the bounded answer.

**Recurrence inventory:** 34 records across 31 videos.

**Practice:** Give a direct answer, then a frame-corrected answer; ask which one remains responsive.

**Self-check:** Can the listener identify both the direct answer and any justified reframing?

**When not to use:** Do not reframe merely to avoid an uncomfortable but legitimate question.

**Delivery evidence:** 0 internally reviewed sample(s); status `no_aligned_internal_sample_for_this_job`.

## `story_or_personal_experience`

**Problem:** Make an idea concrete without turning one life into universal proof.

**Mechanism hypothesis:** Hypothesis: time, place and action can give an abstract point human and temporal scale; an explicit boundary can prevent overgeneralization.

**Repeatable form:** Give a bounded scene; say what it changed or illustrates; state what it cannot prove.

**Recurrence inventory:** 184 records across 121 videos.

**Practice:** Tell a 45-second event, then name the narrow lesson and one limit.

**Self-check:** Can the listener separate the event, the interpretation and the limit?

**When not to use:** Do not disclose another person’s private experience or use trauma as decorative evidence.

**Delivery evidence:** 2 internally reviewed sample(s); status `internal_bounded_review_not_independent`.

## `example_or_case`

**Problem:** Give a general claim a concrete handle without presenting an illustration as proof.

**Mechanism hypothesis:** Hypothesis: a concrete case can make an abstract claim inspectable; returning to the claim helps distinguish illustration from evidence.

**Repeatable form:** State the claim; give one representative case; return to the claim and its evidence boundary.

**Recurrence inventory:** 75 records across 60 videos.

**Practice:** Explain a principle with and without a case; ask which detail made the idea more inspectable.

**Self-check:** Does the case clarify the claim without pretending to establish its prevalence or cause?

**When not to use:** Do not select a vivid outlier to imply a typical pattern without denominators.

**Delivery evidence:** 1 internally reviewed sample(s); status `internal_bounded_review_not_independent`.

## `contrast_or_disagreement`

**Problem:** Make a distinction or change in reasoning visible without escalating disagreement.

**Mechanism hypothesis:** Hypothesis: naming both sides can reduce the listener’s need to infer the replacement claim, but contrast can oversimplify real alternatives.

**Repeatable form:** Name the earlier view; identify what changed; state the present view and remaining overlap.

**Recurrence inventory:** 87 records across 72 videos.

**Practice:** Use ‘I used to think X; Y changed the question; now I think Z,’ then test whether the distinction is fair.

**Self-check:** Can the listener state what changed without turning the other side into a caricature?

**When not to use:** Do not manufacture a false binary or treat a person as an argument to defeat.

**Delivery evidence:** 0 internally reviewed sample(s); status `no_aligned_internal_sample_for_this_job`.

## `correction_or_reframe`

**Problem:** Move from an unhelpful starting frame to a more useful question or category.

**Mechanism hypothesis:** Hypothesis: an explicit frame correction can prevent downstream reasoning from inheriting a false premise, but it can feel evasive if the original concern is ignored.

**Repeatable form:** Acknowledge the starting frame; explain the correction; answer the more useful question.

**Recurrence inventory:** 74 records across 61 videos.

**Practice:** Answer a difficult prompt twice: first literally, then with one transparent frame correction.

**Self-check:** Did you preserve the valid concern inside the revised frame?

**When not to use:** Do not label a challenge a ‘bad question’ simply because it is inconvenient.

**Delivery evidence:** 0 internally reviewed sample(s); status `no_aligned_internal_sample_for_this_job`.

## `repair_or_self_correction`

**Problem:** Restore accuracy or shared understanding after an error, interruption or unclear phrase.

**Mechanism hypothesis:** Hypothesis: a visible repair can update the listener’s model and reduce ambiguity, but excessive self-correction can interrupt the thought.

**Repeatable form:** Mark the error; replace it plainly; continue without over-explaining.

**Recurrence inventory:** 15 records across 14 videos.

**Practice:** Record a short answer with one intentional error and repair it in one sentence.

**Self-check:** Is the corrected version unmistakable, and can the listener continue with you?

**When not to use:** Do not use ‘repair’ theatrically to avoid responsibility for a substantive claim.

**Delivery evidence:** 0 internally reviewed sample(s); status `no_aligned_internal_sample_for_this_job`.

## `qualification_or_boundary`

**Problem:** State a clear point while marking uncertainty, scope or conditions.

**Mechanism hypothesis:** Hypothesis: an explicit qualifier helps locate certainty and revision conditions, but vague hedging can hide the claim rather than calibrate it.

**Repeatable form:** State the main claim; name its evidence status or condition; say what would change it.

**Recurrence inventory:** 111 records across 83 videos.

**Practice:** Convert one absolute sentence into a bounded claim with one observable condition.

**Self-check:** Is the claim clearer and more honest, rather than simply weaker?

**When not to use:** Do not add ornamental uncertainty to a claim that should be checked directly.

**Delivery evidence:** 4 internally reviewed sample(s); status `internal_bounded_review_not_independent`.

## `definition_or_explanation`

**Problem:** Make an unfamiliar idea usable without overextending it.

**Mechanism hypothesis:** Hypothesis: an operational definition reduces ambiguity; naming exclusions can prevent the listener from applying it outside its scope.

**Repeatable form:** Name the term; give its plain meaning; show one example and one boundary.

**Recurrence inventory:** 60 records across 50 videos.

**Practice:** Define a technical term for a newcomer in 20 seconds, then ask them to use it in a new sentence.

**Self-check:** Can the listener use the term and say what it does not mean?

**When not to use:** Do not substitute a slogan, metaphor or credential for a usable definition.

**Delivery evidence:** 0 internally reviewed sample(s); status `no_aligned_internal_sample_for_this_job`.

## `transition_or_structure`

**Problem:** Help a listener follow a change of topic, scale, speaker or reasoning step.

**Mechanism hypothesis:** Hypothesis: a signposted transition lowers navigation cost by showing how the next section connects, but too many labels can make speech mechanical.

**Repeatable form:** Close the current point; name the relationship; enter the next point.

**Recurrence inventory:** 92 records across 74 videos.

**Practice:** Add one spoken bridge between two unrelated ideas and ask a listener to describe the connection.

**Self-check:** Can the listener say why the next point arrived here?

**When not to use:** Do not use a transition to imply a connection that the evidence does not support.

**Delivery evidence:** 0 internally reviewed sample(s); status `no_aligned_internal_sample_for_this_job`.

## `callback_or_repetition`

**Problem:** Give a long exchange continuity or let an earlier image carry changed meaning.

**Mechanism hypothesis:** Hypothesis: a callback can bind distant parts of a talk and make a shift noticeable, but repetition can become empty emphasis.

**Repeatable form:** Introduce a meaningful anchor; return to it with a new application; avoid merely repeating it.

**Recurrence inventory:** 34 records across 32 videos.

**Practice:** Use one image at the opening and return to it at the close with a changed implication.

**Self-check:** What new work does the repeated phrase do the second time?

**When not to use:** Do not repeat a slogan to pressure agreement or simulate evidence.

**Delivery evidence:** 0 internally reviewed sample(s); status `no_aligned_internal_sample_for_this_job`.

## `humor_or_rapport`

**Problem:** Create ease or relational room without making affected people the joke.

**Mechanism hypothesis:** Hypothesis: self-directed or situational humor may lower social pressure and make a difficult turn more approachable, but context and power determine risk.

**Repeatable form:** Use lightness about your own situation or the shared setting; return clearly to the point.

**Recurrence inventory:** 28 records across 23 videos.

**Practice:** Try a self-directed release and a neutral transition after difficult material; check whether either targets someone else.

**Self-check:** Who bears the cost of the joke, and can the listener opt out of laughing?

**When not to use:** Do not use humor to trivialize grief, vulnerability, identity or an unresolved harm.

**Delivery evidence:** 0 internally reviewed sample(s); status `no_aligned_internal_sample_for_this_job`.

## `credit_or_acknowledgment`

**Problem:** Make contribution, support or dependence visible without turning praise into a status performance.

**Mechanism hypothesis:** Hypothesis: specific credit can widen the authorship frame and show the work behind an outcome, but lists can exclude or tokenize people.

**Repeatable form:** Name the contribution; connect it to the result; state what remains owed or unfinished.

**Recurrence inventory:** 81 records across 58 videos.

**Practice:** Replace generic thanks with one observable contribution and its consequence.

**Self-check:** Could the credited person recognize the description as accurate and specific?

**When not to use:** Do not claim to transfer authority through praise while retaining all control.

**Delivery evidence:** 0 internally reviewed sample(s); status `no_aligned_internal_sample_for_this_job`.

## `call_to_action_or_audience_role`

**Problem:** Turn a broad aspiration into a voluntary, bounded next role.

**Mechanism hypothesis:** Hypothesis: naming action, owner, time horizon and limit can make a request evaluable, but a request is not proof that the action is feasible or effective.

**Repeatable form:** Name the problem; offer a specific action and responsible actor; state the limit and consent path.

**Recurrence inventory:** 85 records across 69 videos.

**Practice:** Rewrite ‘we should care’ as one feasible action with an owner, timeframe and non-fit.

**Self-check:** Can the listener say what they may do and what the action cannot solve?

**When not to use:** Do not assign risk, money or care labor without authority, consent or support.

**Delivery evidence:** 2 internally reviewed sample(s); status `internal_bounded_review_not_independent`.

## `persuasion_or_advocacy`

**Problem:** Move a room toward judgment or action while showing evidence, tradeoffs and responsibility.

**Mechanism hypothesis:** Hypothesis: a visible chain from problem to mechanism to responsibility can make advocacy inspectable, but rhetorical force does not establish policy truth or outcomes.

**Repeatable form:** State the stake; show the mechanism; name tradeoffs and accountable actors; invite a bounded choice.

**Recurrence inventory:** 66 records across 54 videos.

**Practice:** Make one advocacy claim with evidence status, opposing concern, responsible actor and review point.

**Self-check:** Can a listener disagree with the proposal while accurately restating it?

**When not to use:** Do not use urgency, fear or moral identity to conceal missing evidence or coercion.

**Delivery evidence:** 0 internally reviewed sample(s); status `no_aligned_internal_sample_for_this_job`.

## `reflection_or_value_frame`

**Problem:** Connect an event or decision to a value without pretending the value settles the facts.

**Mechanism hypothesis:** Hypothesis: an explicit value frame can help a listener understand why a detail matters, but moral language can overrule evidence or disagreement.

**Repeatable form:** Describe the event; name the value it raises; distinguish interpretation from verified fact.

**Recurrence inventory:** 92 records across 71 videos.

**Practice:** Take one personal or public event and state the value interpretation plus one alternative reading.

**Self-check:** Can the listener tell which part is observation, value judgment and open question?

**When not to use:** Do not use a value label to end inquiry or claim moral superiority.

**Delivery evidence:** 0 internally reviewed sample(s); status `no_aligned_internal_sample_for_this_job`.
