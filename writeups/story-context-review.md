## One changed decision and a useful boundary

The childhood-acquaintance case says that **if** acquaintances had been told
about a later opportunity, they **would have** laughed. It does not report
that this conversation happened. Under the existing personally experienced-
event rubric, the target is a counterfactual device, so support changes from
true to false. It may still be useful rhetoric; rejection from this category
does not mean the technique is ineffective or outside the project's interest.

The childhood pledge case does describe a past practice and its significance.
It remains a brief personal-experience fragment, not proof of a full narrative
with conflict and resolution. The combined label must not erase that difference.

Similarly, a scene setup and a single action can belong to a story without
capturing its full boundaries. Future story-level analysis needs complete
episodes and their transitions back to the speaker's broader point.

## Implication for the practice card

The card now distinguishes setup, action, attributed dialogue and brief memory.
Its evidence does not justify claiming that every first-person statement is
a story, that the events are factually verified, or that a delivery feature
improves engagement. Garbled quoted wording is preserved rather than silently
repaired. Source IDs, inspected contexts and decision reasons remain available.
