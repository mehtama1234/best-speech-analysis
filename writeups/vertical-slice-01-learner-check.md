# Vertical slice 01 learner check

Status: protocol drafted; no learners tested yet.

## Purpose

Test whether the five lessons teach a transferable communication choice. This is
not an audience-outcome study: it does not test trust, memory, persuasion,
engagement or action caused by the original speech pattern.

## Five tasks

Each learner receives a new transcript segment that was not used in the lesson
packet, then completes the task without seeing the intended answer.

| Pattern | Recognition task | Transfer task | Misuse/boundary task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Explicit comparison | Identify A, B, consequence and limit. | Produce a two-sentence comparison. | Classify contrast vs story transition, example, qualification or conclusion. |
| Concrete example | Classify a detail as evidence, reported example, hypothetical illustration or irrelevant story. | Connect one case to a general claim. | State what the case does not prove. |
| Bounded story | Classify experienced event, hypothetical, generalized story, counterfactual or history. | Tell a short bounded event and connect it to a point. | Replace disclosure with an anonymous or hypothetical case. |
| Qualification | Classify observation, prediction, interpretation or recommendation. | Add the smallest appropriate qualifier and revision condition. | Rewrite both an overconfident and an over-hedged claim. |
| Conclusion | Recover takeaway, support and limit. | Close a short explanation clearly. | Compare explicit verbal marking with a written or visual recap. |

## Procedure

1. Collect a short baseline: the learner explains one unfamiliar topic for two
   minutes without seeing the pattern labels.
2. Teach one lesson using its evidence, limits, practice task and self-check.
3. Give a new recognition item and record the learner’s label, explanation and
   confidence.
4. Give the transfer task and record a short audio or written response.
5. Ask a second listener to restate the intended change, example connection,
   story point, uncertainty or takeaway.
6. Ask the learner to identify when the pattern should not be used.
7. Repeat the baseline task or a matched task and compare clarity of the stated
   job, evidence/limit handling and listener restatement.

## Scoring rubric

Score each item separately as `0 = missing or misleading`, `1 = partial`, or
`2 = clear and bounded`:

- communication job identified;
- pattern recognized in a new example;
- explanation uses plain language;
- transfer response performs the underlying choice;
- alternative explanation or limit retained;
- misuse or non-fit recognized;
- privacy, accessibility and safety boundary respected;
- second listener can restate the intended point.

Do not average away a safety failure. A lesson is not ready for promotion if the
learner can perform the surface marker but cannot state its boundary, or if the
second listener cannot recover the intended point.

## Required record

Record learner ID or pseudonym, date, lesson version, source examples shown,
new examples used, task responses, rubric scores, learner confidence, second-
listener restatement, accessibility needs, difficulties, facilitator changes
and unresolved disagreements. Store no private story or recording without
consent; allow written, typed, captioned or asynchronous responses.

## Interpretation boundary

Improvement in recognition or transfer supports the lesson’s teachability. It
does not show that the original speaker caused better audience outcomes, nor
that the pattern is universally effective. Compare with a plain-communication
control and preserve non-fit cases.

The fixed [task bank](../research/vertical-slice-01-learner-task-bank.json)
contains 20 draft stimuli: four phases for each pattern (recognition, transfer,
misuse/non-fit and matched control). It has not been piloted.
