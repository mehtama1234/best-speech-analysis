1. provisional-contrast_or_disagreement

Recognition task: State an expected outcome and observed outcome in two sentences, then explain what changed without claiming that the contrast proves a personal or interpersonal motive.

Fixed stimuli shown in this section
new_example_recognition — contrast-recognition-01

A project was expected to take two weeks but took six because a required dependency arrived late. Which response most clearly performs the communication job? A: ‘The team is unreliable.’ B: ‘We planned for two weeks; the dependency arrived in week four, so the schedule moved to six weeks. This does not by itself show why the dependency was late.’ C: ‘Everyone knows this always happens.’

transfer — contrast-transfer-01

Describe one expected-versus-observed difference in an ordinary task using two sentences and one limit. Do not name a person as the cause unless you have separate evidence.

misuse_non_fit — contrast-nonfit-01

A speaker says ‘In short, bring your identification tomorrow’ after explaining a process. Is this primarily an explicit contrast? Explain why or why not.

matched_control — contrast-control-01

Give a plain instruction without using a contrast marker, then explain how it differs from reporting an expected-versus-observed outcome.

2. provisional-example

Recognition task: State one general claim, give one concrete case or imagined scene, explain what the case illustrates, and state what it does not prove.

Fixed stimuli shown in this section
new_example_recognition — example-recognition-01

A speaker says ‘Clear documentation reduces repeated questions. For example, our clinic added a one-page intake guide and saw fewer missing fields in one month.’ What must the speaker still avoid claiming? A: that the case illustrates the claim; B: that one case proves the guide caused the entire change or applies to every clinic; C: that the case has any relevance.

transfer — example-transfer-01

State a general claim, give a labeled observed, reported, hypothetical or illustrative case, connect it to the claim, and state one limit. Use no identifying personal detail.

misuse_non_fit — example-nonfit-01

Someone names one dramatic incident and says it proves an entire group is dangerous. Is this a safe use of an example? Explain the missing boundary.

matched_control — example-control-01

Explain a definition directly without adding a case, then say when a concrete example would improve clarity and when it could distract or expose privacy.

3. provisional-story_or_personal_experience

Recognition task: Choose one communication point, tell one short personal event with time/place/action, explain what it illustrates, and name what your experience cannot establish.

Fixed stimuli shown in this section
new_example_recognition — story-recognition-01

Which response uses bounded personal detail responsibly? A: ‘I once missed a deadline on a group project, so everyone should work exactly as I do.’ B: ‘During one group project, I missed a deadline after misunderstanding the handoff. It taught me to confirm ownership; that experience does not establish the best process for every team.’ C: a detailed story naming a private colleague without consent.

transfer — story-transfer-01

Make one communication point concrete with an anonymous, hypothetical or safely bounded event. State what the event illustrates and what it cannot establish.

misuse_non_fit — story-nonfit-01

You do not want to disclose a personal experience, but a listener needs a clear explanation. Is refusing personal disclosure a communication failure? Give a safer alternative.

matched_control — story-control-01

Explain the same communication point directly without a story, then compare the privacy and clarity tradeoffs.

Self-check
4. provisional-uncertainty_or_qualification

Recognition task: State one claim, label whether it is observed, predicted, interpreted or recommended, add the smallest uncertainty needed, and name what evidence would change your mind.

Fixed stimuli shown in this section
new_example_recognition — uncertainty-recognition-01

Which sentence is most precisely qualified? A: ‘This will definitely solve the problem.’ B: ‘The early data suggest the change may reduce delays, but we need another month of records to test that interpretation.’ C: ‘Maybe perhaps possibly it could sort of help.’

transfer — uncertainty-transfer-01

State one observed, predicted, interpreted or recommended claim. Add the smallest qualifier needed and name what evidence would change your mind.

misuse_non_fit — uncertainty-nonfit-01

A manager uses ‘we cannot know anything’ to avoid answering a question about a documented missed deadline. Is this good uncertainty handling? Explain the accountability problem.

matched_control — uncertainty-control-01

State a fact that needs no hedge, then state a prediction that does. Explain why the qualification differs.

5. provisional-conclusion_or_summary

Recognition task: Explain a small topic, then state the takeaway in one sentence, name the evidence supporting it, and state one limit. Ask a listener to repeat the takeaway without seeing your notes.

Fixed stimuli shown in this section
new_example_recognition — conclusion-recognition-01

After explaining how a shared calendar prevents duplicate bookings, which closing is clearest? A: ‘Anyway.’ B: ‘The takeaway is to assign one owner to the calendar; this helps prevent duplicate bookings but does not replace schedule review.’ C: ‘You should agree with me.’

transfer — conclusion-transfer-01

Explain a small topic, mark one takeaway, name its support, preserve one qualification, and offer a next step.

misuse_non_fit — conclusion-nonfit-01

A speaker ends a complex disagreement by saying ‘there is only one acceptable view’ without addressing the evidence. Is this a responsible summary? Explain what was compressed away.

matched_control — conclusion-control-01

Close a short explanation with an unmarked final sentence, then close it again with a marked takeaway. Compare clarity without claiming listener improvement.
