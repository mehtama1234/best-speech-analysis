# The Speaking Patterns Fieldbook: editorial brief

## Purpose

Help a reader make one part of a presentation clearer. The book is organized around seven practical jobs, such as explaining an unfamiliar idea, answering a hard question and asking for a concrete next step. Its index groups 55 ideas into 22 main themes, 18 smaller speaking moves and 15 more ideas to explore.

The seven chapters group the material by the job a speaker needs to do. Every entry in the 55-entry index explains the idea, gives a short source summary, shows how to try it through an invented before-and-after example, offers ways to adapt it and links to the source analysis. The source summary appears before the practice example so the reader can distinguish what was observed from our suggested use. Several entries examine different choices in the same source. The index links each of the 22 main themes to one detailed entry. The other 33 entries are listed separately because they can support more than one theme. These ideas are not 55 proven rules.

## Writing standard

- Use ordinary words and concrete examples. Explain a necessary technical term the first time it appears.
- Tell the reader what to do, and why that step may help with the stated problem.
- Keep the source, our interpretation and invented practice material distinct.
- Do not invent quotations, motives, events or audience reactions.
- State what the example does not show. A transcript pattern does not prove an effect.
- Give a usable exercise and a specific way to check the draft.
- Avoid advice that depends on imitating a named speaker or disclosing someone else's private story.

## Evidence and release limits

The book uses 17 selected transcript analyses from a larger project that reviewed the full transcripts of 395 videos in a 480-video inventory. It is not a new independent review of all 395. Search counts indicate videos with a text-search match, not confirmed examples. No independent reviewers' answers are included, and the book has not been tested with learners or audiences. Do not call the patterns proven or claim that they improve a talk.

The companion 27-chapter guide comes from a separate project and a different set of 10 YouTube channels. Its first Charisma on Command set contains 16 transcripts. The combined fieldbook includes the companion web and PDF editions and an editor-written comparison linking each of its 27 approaches to a related pattern—or stating that this 55-pattern book has no direct match. The comparison does not merge the two source sets or show that their advice works.

The local reading page, manuscript, print edition and offline bundle should agree. Check source links, caption references, mobile reading, keyboard use, printing and package contents. These checks establish that the files work as delivered; they do not establish that the advice works for every audience.

Commercial use may require further rights and attribution review. Naming a speaker does not imply that person endorses the book. Do not present the current editorial draft as a commercially cleared or empirically validated product.
