# Repeatable communication patterns across the corpus

Status: **provisional synthesis for learning; not a validated best-practice library**.

This guide distills the 18 provisional communication jobs into reusable moves.
It is grounded in 395 full-context transcript reviews, the 18-job cross-video
comparison, 50 retrieved exact phrase forms and a 36-sample delivery-inspection
queue. The corpus shows that a move appears in a speech; it does not prove that
the move caused trust, comprehension, persuasion or action. Independent review,
learner records and listener outcomes remain open.

## The central rule

Borrow the **communication choice**, not the speaker. Keep the job and adapt the
words, relationship, culture, stakes, access needs and authority. Do not copy an
accent, status display, emotional performance, body language, personality or
biographical story.

## The eight recurring moves

### 1. Orient before detail

**Jobs:** opening, transition, closing.

Name the room, question or problem; explain why it matters; preview the route.
At a transition, say what relationship the next point has to the current one.
At a close, return to the point, name the limit or next step, and release the
turn.

**Why it might help:** it may reduce the listener's orientation work. It can
also become padding or premature closure, so test whether the listener can state
the purpose and what remains open.

**Form:** `context → stake → route` and later `point → limit/next step → release`.

### 2. Make an idea inspectable

**Jobs:** story_or_personal_experience, example_or_case, definition_or_explanation,
reflection_or_value_frame.

Move from a bounded scene, case or plain definition to the claim it illustrates.
Separate observation, interpretation and evidence. Say what the example cannot
establish; a vivid case is not a denominator or a causal explanation.

**Form:** `claim → concrete instance → interpretation → boundary`.

### 3. Answer, then frame

**Jobs:** answer_or_response, qualification_or_boundary, definition_or_explanation.

Answer the literal question first. Add a frame only when it makes the answer
more useful, then state the condition, uncertainty or evidence status. A frame
that avoids the question is evasion, not skill.

**Form:** `direct answer → useful frame → condition/limit`.

### 4. Make change visible without manufacturing an enemy

**Jobs:** contrast_or_disagreement, correction_or_reframe.

Name the earlier view fairly, identify what changed, state the present view and
preserve any genuine overlap. For a correction, acknowledge the starting frame,
give the replacement and answer the more useful question.

**Form:** `earlier frame → reason for change → present frame → remaining overlap`.

### 5. Repair in public

**Job:** repair_or_self_correction.

Mark the error, replace it plainly and continue. Do not turn a small correction
into a performance of virtue or use an apology to avoid the substantive issue.

**Form:** `I said X → X is wrong/incomplete because Y → the accurate version is Z`.

### 6. Convert credit into agency and responsibility

**Jobs:** credit_or_acknowledgment, call_to_action_or_audience_role,
persuasion_or_advocacy.

Name who contributed, what remains unfinished, who can act, what support or
authority is required, and what the action cannot solve. A request should not
silently transfer risk, money or care labor to the audience.

**Form:** `name contribution/problem → accountable actor → bounded action →
support, consent and limit`.

### 7. Return to an anchor with a changed use

**Job:** callback_or_repetition; often supports closing and advocacy.

Introduce a meaningful phrase, image or question, then return to it after the
reasoning has changed its meaning or application. Mere repetition can pressure
agreement and is not evidence of understanding.

**Form:** `anchor → development → return with new application`.

### 8. Make the next turn answerable

**Jobs:** question, humor_or_rapport, opening, call_to_action_or_audience_role.

Ask one specific question, offer a genuine opt-out, use lightness only when the
shared setting can bear it, and make the next action or response clear. A
question that corners someone is a demand in disguise.

**Form:** `specific invitation → context → answer/decline path → next turn`.

## Words and phrase forms

The exact phrase inventory contains retrieval evidence, not magic words. Forms
such as `for example`, `in other words`, `the point is`, `what matters`, `rather
than`, `I think`, `it depends`, `we need to`, `you can` and `let's` must be read
in their surrounding transcript. The same form can perform different jobs, and
its recurrence does not establish effectiveness.

Use this order when adapting a phrase:

1. State the job in plain language.
2. Write the meaning in your own words without the source phrase.
3. Choose words appropriate to the listener and relationship.
4. Add the relevant evidence, condition, consent or limit.
5. Ask the listener to restate the point; revise if they infer the wrong job.

The [learner field guide](learner-field-guide.md) contains the 50 exact forms,
train/holdout retrieval counts and retained examples. The [phrase-form
inventory](corpus-phrase-form-inventory.html) contains the navigable evidence.

## Delivery: what to inspect, not what to imitate

For each job, inspect the source clip for:

- pacing, pause placement and turn timing;
- emphasis, pitch movement, loudness and vocal effort;
- gaze, posture, gesture, facial movement and physical orientation;
- whether the speaker yields, interrupts, waits or responds to another person;
- setting, microphone, subtitles, music, camera cuts and audience visibility;
- accessibility consequences: captions, audio clarity, visual dependence,
  sensory load and whether the move works in typed or asynchronous form.

These are observation prompts. The repository currently has 36 raw-media sample
rows and bounded internal frame notes, not independent delivery findings. A
single frame or feature proxy cannot establish a meaningful gesture, deliberate
pause, emotion, intent or audience response. When a feature or visual cue is
missing, record `not_available` or `indeterminate`; do not fill the gap with a
celebrity stereotype.

## A repeatable practice cycle

1. Choose the communication job, not a person to imitate.
2. State the listener problem and the smallest useful outcome.
3. Read one full-context example and identify the words doing the work.
4. Rewrite the move for your own situation, including its limit.
5. Choose accessible delivery options and rehearse in the available modality.
6. Ask a listener for a restatement and what remains unclear; do not treat one
   response as proof of general effectiveness.
7. Record the non-fit case, safety issue or unintended burden.
8. Revise the function, wording or delivery choice and test again.

## Claim ladder

`transcript observation` → `recurrence candidate` → `delivery observation` →
`mechanism hypothesis` → `teachability evidence` → `bounded listener outcome`.

Do not skip levels. The current cards are provisional hypotheses. The promotion
readiness report, validation handoff and [end-to-end goal](end-to-end-goal.md)
show which independent gates remain before any pattern can be called a tested
best practice.
