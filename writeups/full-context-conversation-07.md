## Some answers change the question's scope

Khan's opening asks about change in India. Bezos instead emphasizes favorable
features he perceives as continuing (0–17). Those travel impressions are not
longitudinal measurements or population-wide evidence. Akhtar's scale question
then elicits an explicit denial of foresight, contrasted with a modest forklift
aspiration and attributed workforce figures (18–38). The exaggerated psychiatric
comparison is source humor, not a diagnosis or an appropriate evidentiary standard.

At 39–55, a question about products not sold elicits two distinct cases: a product
said to have failed and categories said to be deliberately excluded. Failure is
not the same as a chosen boundary. Product and catalog claims remain historical
source assertions, not current shopping or business guidance.

## Art and business are combined, not cleanly separated

At 56–72, Khan presents a creative/commercial contrast. The response argues that
the best work can do both, then develops freshness, talent and learning through
fiction (73–117). The talent-friendly studio claim is an aspiration. Praise of
Akhtar is participant acknowledgment, not independent evaluation.

The proposed need for lightness within serious storytelling is an aesthetic
principle asserted by the speaker, not a measured audience response. A hyperbolic
distress example is not evidence of present intent or an observed viewer reaction.
Claims that stories teach or change the world need testing before becoming
general practice principles.

## Competition is clarified, then reframed

Akhtar explicitly clarifies that her question concerns creative competition
(118–122). Bezos acknowledges regret about rejected work, notes favorable decisions
elsewhere and argues that good stories need not diminish other good stories
(123–144). This non-zero-sum creative framing is not a validated claim that
platforms or productions never compete economically.

Importantly, the answer admits that a team of talented people can still make a
poor result. That limits the preceding emphasis on talent. Selection mistakes
and uncertainty remain in the source, rather than being cleaned into a reliable
recipe. Wording at 143 appears corrupted and is not silently reconstructed.

## Passion advice retains disliked work—and depends on control

At 145–185, a question about wasted time becomes a discussion of unavoidable
overhead, delegation and freedom from overscheduling. Disliked tasks are both
accepted and candidates for reduction; the advice is not simply tolerate
everything or eliminate every inconvenience.

The executive-delegation example assumes positional power. Equal clock time
does not establish equal control of one's schedule. Those resources must remain
visible before generalizing the account to people with less autonomy.

Later passion advice at 186–193 coexists with that earlier acceptance of disliked
tasks. Loving the work does not mean every task is enjoyable. The absolute claim
that greatness requires love of work is not established by this conversation.

## One sentence revises the advice that precedes it

At 194–215, gifts are distinguished from choices. The wording initially allows
pride in gifts, then explicitly corrects that to celebration, reserving pride
for earned choices. An extraction that stops before the correction reverses the
speaker's intended distinction. The distinction is normative advice, not a
scientific decomposition of ability and effort.

At 216–218, a future-self question contrasts comfort with service and adventure.
It is a motivating binary, not an exhaustive account of possible lives. Safety,
care obligations and meaningful service can coexist with comfort; the source
does not test those alternatives.

## The ending returns to exploratory time

At 219–233, Khan asks about restarting at the guest's current stage. The answer
shifts to a hypothetical young student, names technological interests and ends
with a bartender joke. The age/stage shift should be preserved. Forecasts are
not current career or investment recommendations, and the joke does not verify
professional skill.

At 234–243, a wish for more time returns to the earlier flexibility theme. The
argument is that efficiency assumes a known destination, while wandering can
support discovery. It is not proof that efficiency is always undesirable or
that unstructured time always produces invention. Requested applause closes the
conversation (244–246); actual response has not been observed.
