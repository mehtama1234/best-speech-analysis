# Delivery learning guide: use the words, inspect the delivery

Status: **practice protocol built from partial delivery evidence; not a validated best-practice library**.

The transcript layer covers all 395 fixed-corpus videos. The delivery layer is
smaller and must stay smaller in its claims: 354 videos have local audio/video,
5 have derived features only, 35 are transcript-only and 1 is video-only. The
current job-level delivery queue contains 36 aligned samples, with 21 bounded
internal observations and no independent delivery observations. This guide
therefore gives a way to learn from delivery without turning a frame, a pitch
proxy or a famous speaker's mannerism into a rule.

## The useful distinction

Practice the communicative job in the transcript; test the delivery choice in
your own situation.

- **Observed wording:** what the retained captions say, with an evidence ID and
  surrounding context.
- **Delivery question:** what to listen or look for in the source clip, if the
  clip is available.
- **Practice hypothesis:** a choice worth trying, not a claim that it works.
- **Accessible equivalent:** a way to perform the same communicative function
  through speech, writing, captions, audio, gesture, gaze or no visual channel.
- **Limit:** what the source and the measurement cannot establish.

Do not imitate accent, pitch, facial expression, posture, status display,
trauma, confidence performance or celebrity identity. Borrow a controllable
choice only when it serves the listener and fits the relationship.

## Five delivery dimensions to test

### 1. Timing and silence

Listen for whether a pause separates clauses, gives a listener time to process
a qualification, yields a turn or merely reflects editing. Try one deliberate
pause before the key distinction, then ask whether the listener heard a
boundary or experienced an awkward gap. Caption gaps and activity measures are
navigation proxies; they do not prove intentional pauses.

### 2. Pace and density

When a sentence contains a new term, example and condition, test a slower pace
or shorter clauses. When the point is familiar, test compression. Compare
listener restatement, not your own feeling of fluency. Words-per-second values
in the repository are alignment proxies and are confounded by captioning,
editing and source format.

### 3. Vocal emphasis and effort

Try changing one variable at a time: stress the contrast word, lower the
volume for a qualification, or keep volume steady while changing the sentence
length. Do not infer confidence, sincerity, emotion or authority from RMS,
pitch or spectral measurements. Record the exact wording and the listener's
restatement separately.

### 4. Turn, orientation and visible action

Look for whether the speaker appears to address a person, room, camera or
screen, and whether a gesture, posture change or gaze shift is visibly aligned
with the words. A single midpoint frame cannot establish movement, intention,
audience response or meaning. If visibility is unavailable, use an equivalent
spoken or written signpost rather than treating visual performance as required.

### 5. Setting, edit and access

Record microphone, captions, music, camera cuts, branding, podium, audience,
lighting and whether the speaker is actually visible. These can create an
impression that belongs to production rather than the speech. Check captions,
transcript, audio-only and text-only versions where possible. A useful delivery
choice must survive the access modes your listeners use.

## Job-by-job delivery questions

| Communication job | Vocal test | Visible/access test |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Can the first sentence orient without rushing? | Is the listener addressed by a usable channel, not merely by camera presence? |
| Closing | Does the final point receive enough space to be retrievable? | Is the release clear without requiring eye contact or applause? |
| Question | Does the intonation and pause leave a real answer path? | Can the listener answer, decline or ask for clarification without reading a gesture? |
| Answer or response | Is the literal answer audible before the reframe? | Does the speaker remain oriented to the actual question rather than performing certainty? |
| Story or personal experience | Are scene, interpretation and limit separated by phrasing or pause? | Are privacy and consent preserved; can the story work without a dramatic performance? |
| Example or case | Is the example marked as illustration rather than proof? | Can the case be followed through captions or text without relying on pointing? |
| Contrast or disagreement | Are the two positions distinct without making the transition sound like attack? | Does posture or gaze avoid turning a person into an opponent? |
| Correction or reframe | Is the valid concern acknowledged before the frame changes? | Is the correction visible in words, not dependent on a dismissive expression? |
| Repair or self-correction | Is the replacement sentence clear and brief? | Can the repair be understood without seeing embarrassment or apology cues? |
| Qualification or boundary | Is uncertainty attached to the exact claim, not spread over everything? | Are conditions available in captions and text, not only in facial cues? |
| Definition or explanation | Does the pace leave room for the term, example and boundary? | Can the concept be learned without a diagram, gesture or visual metaphor? |
| Transition or structure | Is the relationship between sections spoken before the shift? | Does the edit or slide change clarify rather than manufacture continuity? |
| Callback or repetition | Does the return add a changed application rather than volume? | Can a text-only listener identify the anchor and its new use? |
| Humor or rapport | Is the joke optional and followed by a return to the point? | Is the target able to decline; does the meaning survive without audience laughter? |
| Credit or acknowledgment | Are contribution and responsibility distinguishable in the sentence? | Does the visible staging distribute attention rather than perform humility? |
| Call to action or audience role | Is actor, action, resource, consent and limit explicit? | Can someone participate without being pressured by crowd energy or status? |
| Persuasion or advocacy | Are stake, mechanism, tradeoff and evidence separated vocally? | Are urgency and imagery supported by accessible text and a safe opt-out? |
| Reflection or value frame | Is interpretation marked as interpretation? | Can the value be questioned without the speaker's expression closing the exchange? |

These are practice questions, not findings that one delivery style is best.

## A five-minute delivery experiment

1. Choose one job and one retained-caption example from the [corpus findings
   catalog](corpus-findings-catalog.md).
2. Write the job in your own words: listener problem, smallest useful outcome,
   condition and limit.
3. Make two recordings or drafts. Change only one delivery dimension: pause,
   pace, emphasis, turn yield, visible orientation or access mode.
4. Give both versions to a consenting listener in randomized order, or review
   them later without knowing which is the original. Ask for a restatement, the
   unresolved point and any burden or pressure felt.
5. Keep the version that better serves the stated job for this listener and
   context; do not generalize from one response.
6. Log the exact wording, delivery change, media/access mode, listener report,
   non-fit case and revision. A practice log is not an effectiveness result.

## What the current corpus can and cannot teach

It can provide full-context wording, structural sequences, candidate jobs,
phrase retrieval handles and source clips for inspection. It can also provide
limited exploratory measurements and bounded frame notes. It cannot yet prove
that a vocal or nonverbal feature caused comprehension, that a gesture means a
particular emotion, that a speaker intended an effect, or that a lesson works
across people.

The delivery queue and independent review instruments are in the [validation
handoff](validation-handoff.md). The [communication-job delivery
inventory](communication-job-delivery-inventory.md) shows the current samples,
measurements and missingness. The [full delivery proxy index](full-delivery-proxy-index.md)
extends navigation to 364 local feature reports, but remains proxy-only. Use
these artifacts before promoting a delivery claim.
