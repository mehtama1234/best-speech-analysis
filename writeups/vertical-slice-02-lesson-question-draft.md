# Candidate lesson: choose the job before asking the question

Status: draft candidate lesson; not independently reviewed or learner-tested.

Questions in the candidate examples do different work. “I wonder what I could
do?” turns another person’s example into self-reflection. “How about that” is a
short reaction that may establish shared attention. “Why don’t we start right at
the beginning?” opens an interview narrative. The reusable lesson is therefore
not “ask more questions.” It is: name the job, choose the least pressuring form,
and leave room for the appropriate answer or silence.

Practice: write three versions of one transition—an information request, an
invitation to reflect and a narrative-opening question. For each, state who can
answer, what answer is wanted, and what would count as a respectful non-answer.

Non-fit check: identify whether the sentence is actually a question, a rhetorical
reaction, a disguised claim, a transition or a pressure tactic. Do not infer
curiosity, engagement or trust from a question mark or rising intonation alone.
