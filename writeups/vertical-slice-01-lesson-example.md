# Draft lesson: move from the general claim to a concrete case

Status: draft practice hypothesis; not learner-validated.

## Communication job

Help a listener picture what a general claim means by giving a concrete case,
statistic, named instance or imagined scene. Label what the case does and does
not establish.

## Practice task

Choose one abstract claim. Record four short moves:

1. State the claim in plain language.
2. Introduce one concrete case and identify whether it is observed, reported,
   hypothetical or illustrative.
3. Explain which part of the claim the case helps the listener see.
4. State one limit: the case is not automatically representative, causal proof
   or permission to expose someone’s private information.

Template:

> “The general point is **[claim]**. For example, **[case]**. This illustrates
> **[specific connection]**, but it does not by itself prove **[limit]**.”

## Self-check

- Did I distinguish an example from evidence or proof?
- Is the case relevant to the claim rather than merely vivid?
- Did I say whether it is observed, reported, hypothetical or imagined?
- Did I explain the connection instead of leaving the listener to infer it?
- Did I avoid unnecessary names, private details or identifying information?
- Did I state what the case cannot establish?

## When not to use it

Do not add a story or statistic merely to decorate a point. Avoid examples that
invite stereotyping, expose a person without consent, make a medical/legal claim
look settled, or imply that one case represents a whole group. A definition,
qualification or direct instruction may be clearer.

## Learner check

Give the learner a new general claim and ask them to classify a supplied detail
as observed evidence, reported example, hypothetical illustration or irrelevant
story. Then ask them to produce a two-sentence example and have a second listener
state both the intended connection and the stated limit.

This tests recognition, transfer, classification and boundary awareness. It does
not establish that vivid examples improve trust, memory or action.
