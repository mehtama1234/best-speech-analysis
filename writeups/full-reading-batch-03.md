## Cross-source lesson for the evidence model

These cases distinguish three things that can superficially look like a return:

1. Repeated words that may come from editorial excerpt assembly.
2. A speaker's signposted principle that reappears in an objection response.
3. A fictional ending action that contrasts with earlier dialogue about inaction.

The relation type, source of construction and verification need differ. A
recurrence claim should identify whether it belongs to the speaker, conversation,
performance or upload edit. Where that is unresolved, retain the uncertainty
instead of assigning a technique.

The next audiovisual checks should focus on uncertain duplication and turn
boundaries, with continuous clips rather than isolated stills. Independent
annotation and listener tests would be required before claims of agreement or
effectiveness. No legacy practice-card admissions or pause decisions change.
