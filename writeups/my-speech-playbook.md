# My speech playbook

Status: **practical adaptation guide built from transcript-derived hypotheses**.

Use this as a one-page operating card. Choose the job before choosing the
words. Adapt the form to the relationship, stakes and access needs; do not copy
the speaker's accent, personality, status performance or biography. The forms
below are useful starting points, not proven magic phrases.

## The 30-second decision

Before speaking, silently complete:

> The listener needs to ______. The smallest useful outcome is ______. The
> evidence/condition is ______. What remains open is ______.

Then choose one move:

| Situation | Say this in your own words | Check immediately |
|---|---|---|
| You need to orient people | “Here is the situation, why it matters, and the route I’ll take.” | Can they say what this is about? |
| You are asked a hard question | “The direct answer is ____. The part that needs framing is ____.” | Did I answer before reframing? |
| You need to explain an idea | “By ____ I mean ____. For example ____. This does not mean ____.” | Can they distinguish definition, example and limit? |
| You disagree | “I used to see it as ____. ____ changed the question. Now I see ____. We still agree on ____.” | Can they state the change fairly? |
| You made an error | “I said ____. That was incomplete/wrong. The more accurate version is ____.” | Is the correction unmistakable? |
| You need to tell a story | “This happened at ____. It showed me ____. It does not prove ____.” | Did a scene become a lesson without becoming universal proof? |
| You need action | “The problem is ____. ____ can do ____, with ____. This will not solve ____.” | Is actor, support, consent and limit explicit? |
| You need to close | “The point is ____. The next step/remaining uncertainty is ____.“ | Can they repeat the takeaway and open question? |

## The high-value sequence

For most explanations, use:

`answer → example → condition → next turn`

Example:

> “Yes, the proposal can reduce delay. For example, the approval step moves
> from three teams to one. That only holds if the team has authority to make
> the decision. Do you want the implementation risk or the cost next?”

This sequence is more reliable than trying to sound impressive: answer the
literal need, make the claim inspectable, bound it, then make the next turn
answerable.

## Phrase handles, not scripts

Use these only when they perform the job you intend:

- **Definition:** “What I mean by ___ is …” / “In other words …”
- **Example:** “For example …” / “One case is …”
- **Contrast:** “I used to think ___; now I think ___ because …”
- **Qualification:** “This is true when …” / “What would change my view is …”
- **Action:** “You can ___ if ___; the limit is …”
- **Close:** “What matters here is …” / “The next step is …”
- **Question:** “The specific question I’m asking is …; it is fine to pass.”

The same word can signal different jobs. “But” is not a disagreement method;
“I think” is not automatically calibrated uncertainty; a question mark is not
automatically an invitation. Name the function and the boundary.

## Delivery: change one variable

On a second pass, change only one controllable delivery choice:

1. Pause before the distinction or condition.
2. Shorten the clause carrying the new term.
3. Stress the contrast word, not every important word.
4. Yield the turn after a genuine question.
5. Make the equivalent point available in text or captions.

Do not infer confidence, sincerity, emotion or intent from pitch, loudness,
facial movement, posture or a single frame. Ask the listener what they heard,
what remains unclear and whether they felt pressured.

## The two-minute rehearsal

1. Pick one job and write the listener problem in one sentence.
2. Draft the form in your own words, including one condition or limit.
3. Deliver it twice, changing one delivery variable.
4. Ask a consenting listener for: takeaway, unresolved point and pressure/burden.
5. Keep the version that serves this listener and context; record the non-fit
   case instead of generalizing from one response.

## Fast anti-pattern check

Stop and revise if you are:

- using a story as proof of prevalence or causation;
- answering a different question to avoid the real one;
- using urgency or moral identity to hide missing evidence;
- treating a gesture, accent or status object as a required technique;
- closing while a live objection is still unanswered; or
- assigning risk, money or care work without authority, consent or support.
