## Pattern cards

### A. Pair benefit with harm before claiming balance

**Mechanic:** state what a product or policy enables, then name who can be harmed
and what protection is required.

**In this exchange:** connection and expression are placed beside teen harm and
parental difficulty.

**Why it works:** the listener does not have to choose between a total defense and a
total condemnation before the real question begins.

**Guardrail:** “both sides” language is not balance if one side’s harm is minimized
or if no evidence is provided for either side.

### B. Separate three questions that often get blurred

**Mechanic:** distinguish whether harm occurred, whether the organization knew, and
what it did after knowing.

**In this exchange:** the company answer focuses on tools and research; the senator
keeps moving to knowledge, internal action and compensation.

**Reusable form:** “What happened? When did you know? What did you change, who owned
the change and how were affected people helped?”

**Why it works:** it stops a list of future safeguards from answering a question
about past responsibility.

### C. Track the question, not just the topic

**Mechanic:** record the exact question, the answer given, and whether the answer
changed the decision needed.

**In this exchange:** “Who was fired?” and “Were victims compensated?” recur while
the response returns to tools and investment.

**Product rule:** mark a question as answered only when the reply addresses its
actor, action, time, reason or result.

### D. Quote an opponent’s own record with a precise target

**Mechanic:** name the source, quote or summarize the relevant finding, then state
the contradiction that needs explanation.

**In this exchange:** the senator cites reported company research about teen girls
and asks how it fits with the public denial of a link.

**Guardrail:** verify the source, quote, population, time period and causal meaning;
do not turn a reported association into proof of cause.

### E. Distinguish “we study it” from “we accept responsibility”

**Mechanic:** treat research, monitoring and improvement as separate from admission,
remedy and accountability.

**In this exchange:** studying safety and building tools are offered as responses;
the questioning side asks for firing, compensation, apology and personal ownership.

**Reusable form:** “Investigation tells us what happened. Responsibility tells us who
acts, what repair is owed and how the public can check the result.”

### F. Use a benefit list as a shield—and test whether it answers the harm

**Mechanic:** present many safeguards, staff and spending figures; then ask whether
the list answers the specific failure under discussion.

**In this exchange:** a long protection list is followed by questions about known
exposure and individual consequences.

**Use:** this is useful for analyzing institutional speech, not only for attacking
it. A large list can be true and still be nonresponsive.

### G. Compress a moral demand into a decision point

**Mechanic:** after context and examples, ask for a yes/no commitment with a named
owner and a concrete remedy.

**In this exchange:** the compensation-fund question is repeatedly made binary.

**Guardrail:** yes/no pressure can expose evasion but can also erase legal complexity,
uncertain facts or the need to consult affected people. Pair it with a fair process
question.

### H. Let affected people change the room, without using them as props

**Mechanic:** acknowledge families and victims directly, then ask how their presence
changes the duty of the person answering.

**In this exchange:** families are named and shown during the request for apology.

**Guardrail:** people harmed must not be used only as emotional leverage. Give them
voice, privacy, consent and a path to actual remedy.

### I. Separate sympathy, apology, repair and prevention

**Mechanic:** label four different responses: “I am sorry,” “I accept a role,” “I
will repair what is owed,” and “I will reduce future harm.”

**In this exchange:** sympathy and future safety work appear, while the senator asks
for personal responsibility and compensation.

**Why it works:** a warm apology should not be allowed to stand in for repair, and a
compensation plan should not be treated as proof that future harm is solved.

### J. Analyze interruption as a control signal

**Mechanic:** notice when a speaker says “that is not a question,” repeats a question,
or answers while the other person continues; record who controls the frame.

**In this exchange:** the debate moves from prepared statements to interruptions,
repeated questions and competing definitions of “fact” and “answer.”

**Product use:** label interactional control without declaring a winner from tone
alone.

## Product implications

1. **Debate question ledger:** capture each question, exact answer, evidence offered,
   unresolved part, owner and next action.
2. **Responsibility ladder:** separate event, knowledge, decision, ownership, remedy,
   apology, prevention and public verification.
3. **Benefit-harm balance card:** require both product benefits and affected harms,
   with sources, populations and uncertainty.
4. **Evidence quote checker:** store source, exact wording, sample, date, measure and
   whether a claim is correlation or causation before using it in a debate.
5. **Nonresponsive-answer detector:** compare the question’s requested actor, action,
   time and result with the answer; show what remains unanswered.
6. **Commitment compressor:** turn a broad exchange into a clear proposal, owner,
   deadline, remedy, cost, appeal path and review measure.
7. **Affected-person protocol:** guide speakers to acknowledge harm without exposing
   private stories, using victims as props or asking them to perform forgiveness.
8. **Apology-to-repair planner:** turn “sorry” into responsibility, compensation or
   support, prevention, independent checks and a public update.
9. **Interruption map:** visualize turn overlap, repeated questions, frame changes,
   time used and unanswered items without treating volume as truth.
10. **Institutional-answer reader:** compare promises, past actions, resources,
    independent oversight and outcomes rather than rewarding a long list of programs.
11. **Pattern-library tags:** `benefit-with-harm`, `three-responsibility-questions`,
    `question-ledger`, `own-record-quote`, `study-vs-responsibility`,

## Limits and next test

The transcript cannot establish the date, legal setting, speaker roles, source
documents, accuracy of quoted studies, personal knowledge, victim experience or
whether the exchange was edited. The interaction is adversarial and incomplete; it
should not be used as a complete account of social-media safety, mental health,
company responsibility or law. A repeated question can reveal evasion, but it can
also become coercive or prevent a careful answer. A sympathetic apology can be real
and still be insufficient; the reverse is also possible.

Next, compare this exchange with a calm regulatory hearing, a victim-led testimony
and an organization’s written response that includes measures and timelines. Test
whether learners can track question and answer separately, verify quoted evidence,
distinguish association from cause, separate apology from repair, protect affected
people’s agency and identify a concrete accountable commitment. Add independent legal,
research-methods, child-safety, disability, privacy and accessibility review.
