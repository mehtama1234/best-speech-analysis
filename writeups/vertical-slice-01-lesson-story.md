# Draft lesson: use bounded personal detail

Status: draft practice hypothesis; not learner-validated.

## Communication job

Use a short personal event to give an idea human scale without presenting one
person’s experience as universal evidence or a guaranteed route.

## Practice task

Choose a point you want the listener to understand. Record five short moves:

1. State the point before the story, or state what question the story will help
   explore.
2. Give the event’s time, place and one observable action.
3. Name the change, decision or realization inside the event.
4. Connect the event back to the communication point.
5. State what your experience does not prove for other people.

Template:

> “I want to explain **[point]**. When **[time/place]**, **[observable event]**.
> I learned or decided **[bounded meaning]**. This shows **[connection]** for
> me; it does not mean **[universal claim or guarantee]**.”

## Privacy and safety boundary

Use only details you can safely share. Remove names, locations, diagnoses,
identifying family details and other people’s private information unless you
have permission and a real reason to include them. A hypothetical case,
anonymous composite, public example or direct explanation can do the same job.
Do not use trauma disclosure as a credibility requirement.

## Self-check

- Is the event clearly bounded in time and place?
- Did I distinguish what happened from what I inferred?
- Did I connect the event to the point rather than leave it as decoration?
- Did I name what the story cannot prove?
- Did I protect other people’s privacy?
- Could an anonymous or hypothetical example be safer and clearer?
- Did I avoid turning an access-dependent success story into a universal recipe?

## Learner check

Give the learner a new segment and ask them to classify it as an experienced
event, hypothetical scenario, generalized story, counterfactual recollection or
historical explanation. Then ask them to produce a short bounded story and have
a second listener identify the event, intended point and stated limit. A second
round should replace personal disclosure with an anonymous or hypothetical
example and compare clarity and safety.

This tests recognition, transfer, privacy judgment and boundary awareness. It
does not establish that personal stories improve trust, memory or action.
