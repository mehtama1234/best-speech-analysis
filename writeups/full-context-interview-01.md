## What the larger context changes

The earlier sample correctly found a particular encounter followed by a broader
interpretation and a return to that encounter. Reading the entire retained
transcript reveals a stronger attribution caveat: this interview repeatedly
develops ideas through prompts, responses, assent and another participant's
reframing. It is not sound to label the entire sequence one person's prepared
storytelling technique.

## The anchor-return episode, unpacked

At captions 157–169, a participant narrates an encounter with a bereaved young
woman. Captions 170–178 broaden from encounters to the personal stakes of
historical issues. A marked turn at 179 introduces another participant's visit
and national interpretation. A subsequent turn at 193 discusses collective
sacrifice before explicitly returning to the young woman at 199–209 and
developing mutual obligation.

The useful relationship is therefore:

```text
prompt → particular encounter → broader interpretation
       → another participant's perspective
       → explicit return to the individual → broader obligation
```

This refines—not invalidates—the earlier anchor-return observation. The
unmeasured question is whether a listener benefits from the return. The
attribution question is whether to analyze an individual's turn, a sequence of
turns, or the edited interview as a whole. Counting the sequence as independent
examples for each participant would inflate recurrence.

## Two nearby forms that should not be collapsed

The pre-operation-call answer at 94–127 also emphasizes people rather than
abstractions, but it is not the same narrated encounter. That is thematic
continuity, not the same anchor returning. At 370–385, closing thanks returns
to the earlier stated basis for decision confidence at 71–80. That is a
different return: to an explanation or evaluation rather than a story image.

A future comparison should code these separately: same individual/event
revisited, same abstract theme recurring, and earlier explanation restated.
It should also record whether a prompt supplied the frame and whether the
return crosses turns. None is automatically evidence of effective persuasion.

## Practice as an analytical exercise—not a proven prescription

Take an interview passage and mark who introduces the example, who broadens
its meaning, and who returns to it. Then summarize the relationship without
attributing the whole structure to one speaker. Compare with a monologue and
an interview episode that does not return to an earlier example.
