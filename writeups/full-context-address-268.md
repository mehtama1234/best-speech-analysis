## Pattern cards
### A. Receive applause, then make the room playful
The host accepts recognition but immediately shares the event with the guest and audience. A small story or physical joke lowers distance and creates permission for informal answers.
### B. Introduce status, then hand over the microphone
Give context about the guest’s work, but do not keep speaking for them. A useful introduction increases curiosity and then disappears.
### C. Ask a broad question that invites a specific origin
“How did it start?” allows a speaker to choose the first meaningful turning point. Follow-up questions can then organize the story without forcing a success formula.
### D. Build a career from accidents without erasing choice
One line, a back injury, a cast shortage and a move can become a path. Name the accident, the decision made in response, the cost and the later choice to continue.
### E. Use self-correction as warmth
The guest shifts from flirtation to apology, intelligence jokes and self-mockery. Small corrections keep confidence from becoming distance; the joke should not target a vulnerable person.
### F. Put art before celebrity
The claim that the art matters more than the artist redirects attention from personality to what is made. This can be a useful way to discuss work without demanding that audiences worship the maker.
### G. Make the next challenge the hardest one
Past success is treated as completed. The question becomes what remains difficult, which keeps a long career open to learning rather than turning it into a museum.
### H. Turn personal interest into public infrastructure
A love of visual effects, sports or children becomes a reason to build a department, team or project. The move connects private enthusiasm to a system that other people can use.
### I. Use a failure story to show the brink, not promise a turnaround
The speaker names cost, bad performance and uncertainty before saying things can change. Persistence is presented as a personal choice with risk, not a guarantee.
### J. Ask for a specific system improvement
Broad praise for education becomes a request for access, fun, reach and rural or underserved delivery. Specific requests give a powerful organization something to answer.
### K. Keep the stage open when someone loses capacity
When a speaker loses their voice, the group changes the format and keeps them included. Accommodation becomes part of entertainment rather than a reason to remove the person.
### L. Answer fame with ordinary people
The speaker names parents and famous figures, then spends the most emotional detail on workers who wake early, travel, cook and care for children. Ordinary effort becomes a source of dignity.
### M. Borrow imperfect public behavior for creative energy
Bad dancing, odd videos and “epic fails” become material. The pattern welcomes participation without requiring mastery, but it must protect people from unwanted exposure.
### N. End by releasing the room
Thank the audience, name the practical constraint and stop. A good host does not stretch a successful interaction until it loses energy.
## Product implications
1. **Shared-stage interviewer:** coach a host to introduce context, ask a broad question, follow the answer and return the microphone.
2. **Status-to-warmth opener:** generate playful, consent-safe ways to reduce distance after a formal introduction.
3. **Origin-story extractor:** turn “how did it start?” into turning point, accident, response, cost, choice and present direction.
4. **Accident-to-path mapper:** separate luck, injury, loss, access, decision and persistence so users do not copy a celebrity’s story as a recipe.
5. **Art-before-artist prompt:** help a speaker discuss the work, craft and audience effect without erasing labor or excusing harm by the maker.
6. **Next-challenge planner:** close a project, name what was learned and define the next meaningful challenge.
7. **Interest-to-infrastructure builder:** connect a personal interest to a team, tool, institution, service or public benefit.
8. **Brink-and-risk ledger:** record cost, uncertainty, evidence, stop conditions and reasons for continuing before advising persistence.
9. **Specific-system-request editor:** turn broad praise or concern into a request with users, place, access barrier, owner, resources and measure.
10. **Live-accommodation mode:** detect voice, hearing, language or energy constraints and offer alternate formats that keep the person in the interaction.
11. **Ordinary-effort archive:** collect consented stories of work, care, travel and persistence without treating hardship as entertainment.
12. **Imperfect-participation library:** use low-stakes, opt-in examples of learning and play while protecting privacy and dignity.
13. **Release-the-room timer:** help hosts recognize a natural close, thank participants and hand people to the next activity.
