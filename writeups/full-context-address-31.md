## Changing dreams is paired with work and selection

The first two sections move from core values/flaws to permission for dreams
to evolve (48–76). The career-change account illustrates that distinction;
it does not establish that every choice produces the desired result.

Unplanned opportunities are described alongside effort to make use of them
(77–94). The ambition section then supplies a nonharm caveat before a rapid
international-travel example (95–125). The anecdote reports sleep loss and
extra preparation. It cannot establish that costs vanish or that copying the
schedule is safe or feasible for everyone.

The next section explicitly includes declining opportunities that did not fit
(126–140). Thus the broad desire for everything is not accurately summarized
as accepting every offer. Preserve that selection alongside the expansive rhetoric.

## Failure is processed, not simply dismissed

An audience question leads to a claimed raised-hand response (141–145), but
no visual observation was made here. The account of disappointment names
family and friends as support, then moves to getting up and analyzing failure
(146–168). The instruction is not to ignore what happened.

The claim that failure is necessary to truly enjoy success remains a strong

## Risk is explicitly qualified

The risk section distinguishes educated, calculated choices from thrill-seeking
(169–190). Career examples support the speaker's retrospective judgment, but
the future is expressly unknown. Crediting full effort does not independently
demonstrate that effort caused the favorable outcomes or makes future risks safe.

The following short rule names the influence of friends, family and colleagues
(191–195). That matters alongside earlier claims of owning the journey: the
text includes dependence on others rather than a wholly solitary achievement.
