# Seven-day speech practice path

Status: **self-guided practice using provisional, transcript-derived lessons**.

This path turns the 18 communication jobs into one week of usable practice.
It is not a performance guarantee, a personality-imitation program or
independent validation. Keep the wording in your own voice and treat every
result as one observation about one attempt.

## The loop for every day

1. Choose one real situation and write the listener problem in one sentence.
2. Read the linked job card and one timestamped example in the [learner field
   guide](learner-field-guide.md).
3. Record two versions of a 60–90 second answer: ordinary version, then the
   adapted structure.
4. Ask one listener to answer: “What was my point, and what remains unclear?”
   If no listener is available, wait 10 minutes and write the restatement you
   think a listener would give; mark it as self-check, not listener evidence.
5. Keep the move only if it makes the point clearer without inflating the
   evidence, pressure or certainty of the claim.

## Day 1 — orient and release

**Jobs:** opening, closing.

**Opening form:** name the room or question → say why it matters → preview the
route.

**Closing form:** return to the point → name the limit or next step → release
the turn.

**Exercise:** Explain a current project in 90 seconds. In version two, make the
purpose clear in the first sentence and end with one next step plus one thing
you are not claiming.

**Check:** Can the listener state the purpose, takeaway and open question
without repeating your exact words?

**Do not use:** a formal opening when the other person has asked a fast,
narrow question; a closing to avoid a live objection.

## Day 2 — make the abstract inspectable

**Jobs:** story or personal experience, example or case.

**Story form:** bounded scene → what it illustrates → what it cannot prove.

**Example form:** claim → one case → return to the claim and evidence limit.

**Exercise:** Explain a principle twice: once abstractly and once with a
45-second scene or case. Label which details are observed, remembered,
reported or imagined.

**Check:** Can the listener separate the event from your interpretation and
from the general claim?

**Do not use:** another person’s private experience without permission, or a
vivid outlier as if it established prevalence.

## Day 3 — disagree without hiding the change

**Jobs:** contrast or disagreement, correction or reframe.

**Contrast form:** earlier frame → what changed → present frame → remaining
overlap.

**Reframe form:** acknowledge the starting question → explain the correction →
answer the more useful question.

**Exercise:** Take a disagreement you actually have. State the other view
fairly, name the evidence or experience that changed your view, then state
your current position and one point of overlap.

**Check:** Could the other person recognize their view in your summary?

**Do not use:** a false binary, a caricature or “that is the wrong question”
as a shortcut around a valid concern.

## Day 4 — calibrate certainty

**Jobs:** qualification or boundary, definition or explanation.

**Qualification form:** claim → evidence status or condition → what would
change it.

**Definition form:** term → plain meaning → example → boundary.

**Exercise:** Take one strong opinion and rewrite it in three layers: verified
fact, current interpretation and preference. Add one condition under which you
would revise it.

**Check:** Can the listener tell what you know, infer and prefer?

**Do not use:** “I think,” “maybe” or “it depends” as decorative softness when
the claim should instead be checked or answered directly.

## Day 5 — make the next turn answerable

**Jobs:** question, answer or response, transition or structure.

**Question form:** specific question → relevant context → answer or opt-out
path.

**Answer form:** literal answer → useful frame if needed → bounded answer.

**Exercise:** Convert one broad question into a question another person can
answer, decline or clarify. Then answer a difficult question directly before
adding any reframe.

**Check:** Can the listener identify the question, the direct answer and the
reason for the added frame?

**Do not use:** rhetorical questions to corner someone or reframing to evade
the literal point.

## Day 6 — repair, credit and action

**Jobs:** repair or self-correction, credit or acknowledgment, call to action
or audience role.

**Repair form:** “I said X” → “X is incomplete/wrong because…” → accurate Z.

**Credit form:** contribution → result → what remains owed or unfinished.

**Action form:** problem → specific actor and action → support, consent and
limit.

**Exercise:** Record an answer containing one intentional minor error, repair
it once, credit someone whose work made your result possible, and end with one
action that has a named owner.

**Check:** Is the correction unmistakable? Is the credit observable? Can the
actor reasonably consent and carry out the action?

**Do not use:** theatrical self-correction, praise that hides control, or an
urgent request that assigns money, danger or care labor without support.

## Day 7 — integrate and transfer

**Jobs:** callback or repetition, humor or rapport, persuasion or advocacy,
reflection or value frame.

**Exercise:** Give a three-minute explanation using this route:

`orient → concrete scene/example → claim → qualification → fair contrast →
bounded action → callback close`

Use one light line only if it is about your own situation or the shared room;
then return to the point. Mark which sentence is evidence, interpretation,
value judgment and request.

**Final check:** Ask the listener for (a) the one-sentence takeaway, (b) the
claim they distrust or need evidence for, (c) the next action, and (d) the
part that felt pressuring or unclear. Revise once using that feedback.

## What to record

Keep a small log with: date, situation, job, exact form you adapted, listener
restatement or self-check, uncertainty, accessibility needs, what you changed
and the non-fit case. Do not record private listener details without consent.

The [communication-job practice surface](communication-job-practice.html) and
[learner field guide](learner-field-guide.md) provide the 18-card versions of
these exercises. The [end-to-end goal](end-to-end-goal.md) explains why these
practice notes are not yet learner-validation or listener-outcome evidence.
