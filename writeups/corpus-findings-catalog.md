## How to use this tomorrow

1. Pick the job matching the moment: open, explain, disagree, ask, tell a story, repair, or close.
2. Say the copyable form once in your own words; do not imitate the speaker’s identity or accent.
3. Record a 60–120 second attempt, then ask one listener what they understood and where they lost the thread.
4. Keep the move only if it improves clarity without making the claim stronger than the evidence.

### `opening`

**What it solves:** Give the listener a reason and orientation for the exchange.

**Why it may work:** Hypothesis: an opening can reduce orientation cost by naming the situation, relationship or question before detail arrives.

**Copyable form:** Name the room or question; state why it matters; preview the route.

**Practice:** Open the same two-minute explanation with context, then without it; ask a listener what they think the talk is for.

**Self-check:** Can a listener state the situation and purpose without guessing?

**Do not force it when:** Do not force a formal opening into a fast response or an already shared conversation.

### `closing`

**What it solves:** End without leaving the main point or next step diffuse.

**Why it may work:** Hypothesis: a bounded close can compress the route and give the listener a retrievable takeaway, but repetition alone is not evidence of understanding.

**Copyable form:** Return to the point; name the limit or next step; release the turn.

**Practice:** Close a short explanation in one sentence, then ask a listener to restate the takeaway and what remains open.

**Self-check:** Does the close add orientation rather than merely announce that the talk is ending?

### `humor_or_rapport`

**Why it may work:** Hypothesis: self-directed or situational humor may lower social pressure and make a difficult turn more approachable, but context and power determine risk.

**Copyable form:** Use lightness about your own situation or the shared setting; return clearly to the point.

**Practice:** Try a self-directed release and a neutral transition after difficult material; check whether either targets someone else.

**Self-check:** Who bears the cost of the joke, and can the listener opt out of laughing?

## What is still not established

The corpus has not yet established which moves work best, whether delivery changes the effect, whether learners can perform them, or whether listeners experience better outcomes. Those require independent function/recurrence review, audiovisual delivery review, learner records and locked listener stimulus tests. Keep those claims separate from this catalog.
